# Country Liquor & Duty-Free Reference

General-purpose content for any traveler (not gay-tour specific) — useful as a companion section in any travel guide PDF this skill produces. Pair with `city-hotels.md` for the lodging side.

## Korea import duty-free allowance (relevant when traveler is returning to Korea)
As of March 21, 2025, the bottle-count cap was removed. Current rule: **total volume ≤2L AND total price ≤$400 USD**, no limit on number of bottles. Both conditions must hold — if either is exceeded, the excess portion (not just the literal excess volume) becomes taxable, and in some cases the entire bottle's value becomes taxable if that single bottle alone breaks either limit. Allowance applies per-traveler (age 19+); minors get zero alcohol allowance regardless of accompanying adult's room left in their own allowance. Always keep receipts — undeclared excess carries a 40-60% penalty surcharge on top of tax; voluntary declaration gets a 30% discount (capped). This allowance is independent of the general $800 duty-free allowance (alcohol/tobacco/perfume have their own separate caps).

Practical implication for "what to buy" planning: prioritize total $ value and total mL jointly, not just one or the other. A single $500 bottle blows the $400 cap even if it's under 2L. A 3-bottle 2.1L combo blows the 2L cap even if cheap. Multi-bottle combos that split a reasonable budget across 2-3 bottles tend to clear both caps with room to spare.

## 🇯🇵 Japan
**Signature spirit:** Japanese whisky (Yamazaki, Hakushu, Hibiki, Nikka).
**Reality check:** Yamazaki 12/18yo and Hakushu 12yo are extremely scarce even in Japan — retail price has detached from the official ¥15,000 (12yo) / ¥50,000 (18yo) MSRP, often selling 2x+ MSRP when found at all, with frequent simple out-of-stock. Treat finding either as a lucky win, not a plan.
**Reliable buys:** Yamazaki Distiller's Reserve (NAS) ¥9,000, Hakushu Distiller's Reserve (NAS) ¥9,000 — these are the most consistently available bottles in the actual Yamazaki/Hakushu lineage. Suntory Kakubin ¥1,640 and Suntory Old ¥2,180 are cheap, widely available, not exported much (souvenir value). Nikka Yoichi Grande is a reliable duty-free-exclusive single malt around ¥10,000.
**Where to shop:** Convenience-located liquor megastores (e.g. Liquor Mountain chain) tend to have better staff knowledge and stock turnover than airport duty-free for the rare bottles; airport duty-free for Yamazaki/Hakushu standard lineup is often picked clean.
**2L/$400 combo ideas:** Yamazaki DR + Hakushu DR (1.4L, ~¥18,000) is the safe pick; swap in Lagavulin 16 or Glenfarclas 105 for variety since neither counts against "is it actually Yamazaki" disappointment.

## 🇹🇼 Taiwan
**Signature spirits:** Kavalan whisky (Taiwanese single malt, internationally awarded since 2010), Kinmen Kaoliang (金門高粱酒, sorghum liquor, 38-58% ABV depending on grade).
**Why it's good value:** Taiwan taxes imported spirits by volume/ABV rather than ad valorem, and tariffs are comparatively low — so even Scotch whisky can be cheaper in Taiwan than in the UK itself. Kavalan is domestically produced so pricing is even more favorable; the flagship Solist series runs roughly NT$3,200 (700ml) to NT$4,400 (1L) — noticeably cheaper than equivalent imported single malts.
**Where to shop:** Duty-free airport stores carry the Kavalan core lineup; specialty liquor shops in the city (e.g. independently-run stores near Ximending/Taipei 101) often have better prices and rarer bottlings than duty-free, plus tastings. The Kavalan Concept Store at Taipei 101 B1 sells the full range including store-exclusive bottlings.
**Souvenir-tier:** Kinmen Kaoliang is the "must-bring-home" item for anyone into strong spirits — distinct sorghum flavor, not widely available outside Taiwan/Chinese diaspora markets.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand
**Signature spirits:** SangSom (rum, Thailand's best-known local spirit), Mekhong ("Thai whisky," technically a rum-whisky hybrid), Regency (brandy).
**Price point:** Budget-tier — these are mass-market local spirits, not premium buys. Good for the novelty/souvenir factor or for travelers who enjoy them locally (e.g. Thai bucket cocktails use SangSom as a base) rather than as a serious whisky-collector purchase.
**Where to shop:** King Power duty-free dominates Thai duty-free retail — both the in-town King Power Complex (browse 30 days before departure, pick up at airport) and the Suvarnabhumi/Don Mueang airport locations. International liquor (Scotch, Japanese whisky, cognac) is also available at King Power and is sometimes competitively priced versus other Asian duty-free hubs — worth a price comparison if the traveler is hunting for non-Thai spirits rather than local ones.
**Note:** Thailand's own duty-free allowance for *incoming* visitors is separate from Korea's outgoing-to-Korea allowance — only the Korea-side 2L/$400 rule matters for what the traveler can bring home tax-free.

## 🇨🇳 China (Shanghai / Chengdu)
**Signature spirit:** Baijiu (白酒) — sorghum-based, very high ABV (40-65%), an acquired taste for non-Chinese palates but culturally significant. Premium brands (Moutai 茅台, Wuliangye 五粮液) carry serious collector/gifting value and price tags — Moutai flagship bottles can run far above the $400 cap on their own, so budget-conscious travelers should target mid-tier baijiu or skip premium baijiu for the Korea-bound allowance entirely.
**Cognac note:** Chinese duty-free/retail also has a notably strong cognac market (a major import category) — worth checking if the traveler prefers cognac over baijiu, since selection and sometimes pricing in China can be competitive.
**Where to shop:** City duty-free (e.g. CDFG operations) plus large liquor retail in shopping districts; airport duty-free for international travelers covers both baijiu and major international spirits.
**Practical tip for Korea-bound:** A mid-tier baijiu bottle (sub-$100, 500-750ml) paired with one more bottle of something else fits the 2L/$400 cap comfortably and gives a genuinely distinctive souvenir most travelers don't bring home.

## General duty-free shopping principles (apply across all countries)
1. Compare in-town duty-free (browse-ahead, pick-up-at-airport, e.g. King Power in Bangkok) vs airport duty-free — in-town is often less picked-over and has more time to browse without a flight looming.
2. For *country-specific specialty spirits* (Kavalan, baijiu, Japanese whisky), local liquor retail outside duty-free sometimes beats duty-free on price and selection, especially for rare/allocated bottlings — duty-free tends to carry only the standard lineup.
3. Always compute the Korea-bound 2L/$400 math before committing to a "dream bottle" — a single allocated/rare bottle can eat the entire budget allowance, leaving nothing for a second bottle.
4. Carry receipts for any bottle that might approach the value cap — undeclared/unprovable value gets assessed at the customs officer's judgment, typically unfavorably.
